# Fukanzazengi — Week One Reading Notes

Read Dōgen's text once this week. Do not try to master every phrase.

## Notice the architecture

Dōgen does not present zazen as a relaxation exercise or as one meditation technique among many. He first challenges the assumption that the Way is something distant that must be acquired, and then gives remarkably concrete bodily instructions.

That combination matters: **nothing is lacking, yet the form is exacting.**

## Watch for four things

1. **No gaining idea.** Notice every place where the text undermines the project of obtaining enlightenment as a possession.
2. **Embodied precision.** Posture, mouth, eyes, breathing, and the settling movement are not incidental decoration.
3. **Thinking / not-thinking / non-thinking.** Do not reduce this to blanking the mind. We will study hishiryō separately.
4. **Practice as the Way itself.** Ask why Dōgen can demand disciplined sitting if realization is not a prize produced at the end.

## One question after reading

> If zazen is not a machine for manufacturing a better state, what makes sincere practice different from merely sitting around?

Do not force an answer. Keep the question available for later study.

## Week One restraint

During the sitting itself, do not mentally quote these notes or analyze Dōgen. Study happens in study time. Zazen happens in zazen time.
